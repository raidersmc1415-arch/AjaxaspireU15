Message from Coach
Club Identity · Renzo Ulivieri (Coverciano)
IF WE HAVE TO SIT DEEP AND COUNTER-ATTACK, WE ARE ITALIAN.
IF WE WIN THE BALL WITH NO IMMEDIATE COUNTER, WE ARE SPANISH.
IF THE FASTEST SOLUTION IS FORWARD, WE ARE GERMAN.
This is our identity. We don't play one way; we read the game and solve the problem in front of us. Our three build-out plans, our defensive spectrum, and our attacking patterns all flow from this principle. Smart football. Fluid football.

Our Game Model

Tap any principle for coaching detail

We play proactive, intelligent football. In possession we build with purpose: going around, through, or over the opponent's pressure depending on what the game presents. We value technical quality, spatial awareness, and bravery on the ball. Out of possession, every player is a defender from the moment we lose it. Our system demands high effort, high standards, and constant communication, and above all, the ability to read the game and make the right decision.

In Possession
Attack
01 Forward First, Secure and Switch
▶
Our first look is always forward. If it's on, play it. "Patience creates the moment, bravery takes it." But forward first does NOT mean force it. When the forward ball isn't there, we secure it: play the negative or lateral pass, keep possession, and switch the point of attack to find a new opening. We're not in a hurry to go forward; we're in a hurry to go forward correctly. This holds in every system we play. Example: the #6 (or the checking-in mid) receives and turns. Is the forward pass on? If yes, go. If not, secure it back or square it and switch the field to attack the other side.
02 Avoid Standing in the Same Line
▶
Move to create an advantage. Every run either opens space for you or opens space for a teammate. If two of us are on the same horizontal line, the defense only has to deal with one problem instead of two, so we never make it that easy for them.
03 See It, Take It
▶
When space opens behind their line, take it. Be brave in 1v1s. "Hesitation is the only mistake." A wrong decision made with conviction is more coachable than a right decision made too late.
04 Build Patient. Finish Ruthless.
▶
Quality over volume. We don't force chances; we earn them. Calm and good decision-making keeps the ball, recycles possession, moves the defense to our will, and creates goal scoring opportunities. When the moment comes, we finish it.
Out of Possession
Defend
01 Everyone Defends
▶
Every player is a defender the moment we lose the ball. This is a mentality, not a position: strikers defend, wingers defend, everyone defends. There is no "that's not my job" on this team.
02 Five Seconds. Hunt.
▶
The moment we lose the ball is the moment we win it back. The opponent is most vulnerable in the first five seconds after receiving, before they've had a chance to look up and organize. Counter-press immediately, as a group, not as one player chasing alone.
03 Defend as One Body
▶
Get compact. Shrink the field. No daylight between the lines. We don't defend as individuals; we defend as a connected unit, moving together like one shape on the field.
04 Know Your Job: Pressure, Cover, or Balance
▶
Every player out of possession is always one of three things: the pressure (closing down the ball), the cover (backing up the presser), or the balance (protecting the space everyone else has left). Read the game and know which one you are right now.
Our Attacking Spectrum
▶
Build Out (Constructive)
Patient and read-based. GK and back four progress the ball through the press using Plan A, B, or C depending on how the opponent sets up. We're never forced and never rushed. The picture tells us which plan to use.
Combination Play (Progression)
Quick passing through the lines. 8 and 10 occupy different vertical planes, never flat, to create passing triangles. 7 and 11 provide width. 6 anchors and recycles. This is where most of our possession lives. The #2 and #3 recognize when to join the attack on the overlap, reading the trigger when a winger cuts inside and the wide channel opens.
Direct Attack (Penetration)
5-second urgency to quickly expand and stretch the field: #7 and #11 create width, #9 creates depth. The first line-breaking pass goes immediately. We don't wait to set up; we exploit the moment before they recover their shape.
Our Defensive Spectrum
▶
High Press
Coordinated, trigger-based. We press as a unit when the cue fires: bad first touch, slow pass, opponent facing their own goal, or GK with the ball. Half-pressing is worse than not pressing. Commit fully or hold your shape.
Mid Block (4-4-2)
Zonal compactness. Two banks connected and staggered: we hold our shape and force play into predictable corridors. Don't chase runners out of your zone. Move as a unit. Squeeze when the trigger fires on the touchline.
Emergency / Box Defense
When we're defending our 18, zonal is over. Grab your mark. Emergency defending: every player accounts for a body. Clear your lines. Live to play another day. No heroics, defend first.
Player Standards
What We Do
  • Hold yourself and your teammates to a high standard, with kindness
  • Bring energy to every session. High fives, encouragement, focus
  • Be coachable. Listen, ask questions, apply the feedback
  • Show up on time, ready: cleats on, phone away
  • Support teammates on and off the field. No one stands alone
  • Take ownership of your development. The work you do away from training defines you
What We Don't Do
  • Gossip, negativity, or poor conduct on social media; it reflects on all of us
  • Direct frustration at teammates. Compete with each other, not against each other
  • Confuse being loud with being a leader. Leadership is demonstrated, not declared
  • Use poor body language. How you carry yourself sends a message to your team
  • Make excuses or point fingers. Take accountability and move forward
  • Be casual about punctuality. Being late communicates that your time matters more than everyone else's

Tactical Setup
Positions & Roles

Double Pivot Holding Midfielders · #6 & #8 · 4-2-3-1

The Engine Room of the 4-2-3-1: Just like our 4-4-2, we play with two holding midfielders (#6 and #8) in front of the back four, which means our build-out patterns (Plan A, Plan B, Plan C) stay identical across both systems.

In Possession (Ball-Side Pivot & Equilibrium): When the ball is on your side of the field, check in to receive on the half-turn (or act as the decoy to free the fullback in Plan A). When one pivot steps forward to link with the #10 or crash the box (#8), the other MUST hold behind the ball (#6) as the central anchor.

Out of Possession: Form a connected two-player shield directly in front of the center backs (#4 and #5). Protect the half-spaces, deny passes into the opponent's #10 or strikers, and win every second ball.

Attacking Midfielder (The Window) · #10 · 4-2-3-1

In Possession (Find the Window): You are the central connector between our double pivot (#6 and #8) and our striker (#9). Operate in the "window" (pocket) behind the opponent's midfield line. In Build Out Plan A, B, and C, you drop into the window just like in the 4-4-2—receiving on the half-turn to slip #9, #7, or #11 in behind, or bouncing the ball to an advancing pivot.

Combination & Box Arrival: Never stand still on the same line as #9. Float into half-spaces to create 3v2 overloads with the wide players, and arrive in the box on every cross.

Out of Possession: You have a critical defensive reference: screen or lock onto the opponent's #6 (holding mid) so they cannot build through the center, and step up alongside #9 when we trigger the press or form our mid-block.

Wide Attacking Midfielders · #7 & #11 · 4-2-3-1

Higher Starting Position: In the 4-2-3-1, you start higher than in a flat 4-4-2—forming the line of three with the #10 behind the #9.

In Possession: Stretch the opponent's back line horizontally. When you receive wide, attack your fullback 1v1 or drive inside into the half-space to combine with the #10 (which triggers your fullback #2 or #3 to overlap around you). When the ball is on the opposite flank, tuck inside to attack the back post and penalty spot.

Out of Possession: Drop in level with the double pivot (#6 and #8) to form a compact midfield bank of four (4-4-1-1 / 4-4-2 defensive block). Track their fullback all the way and help lock the touchline trap.

Center Forward · #9 · 4-2-3-1

In Possession (Pin & Penetrate): As the lone striker at the top of the 4-2-3-1, your first job is to occupy and pin both opponent center backs. By holding the line high, you create the central window underneath you for the #10 to receive. Threaten the channels and space in behind on every forward look, and when you check to feet, hold the ball up cleanly to bring #10, #7, and #11 into the attack.

Out of Possession: You set the angle of our press. Curve your pressing run between the center backs to split the field in half, while the #10 locks onto their holding mid behind you.

Goalkeeper · #1 · 4-4-2

In Possession: High-level distribution is your weapon. Direct clip passes into the fullbacks and wide mids, and long throws to launch our counters. When we build short, be a calm outlet. Play with purpose: in this system we look forward first, so hit the forward option when it's on.

Out of Possession: Command the box on crosses. This system invites wide service, so own your area. Sweep the space behind the back line when we squeeze up. Organize the two banks of four constantly; your voice sets the shape and the compactness.

Center Backs · #4 & #5 · 4-4-2

In Possession: Composed on the ball. Look for the diagonal into the forwards or the wide outlet first. When the ball goes wide and a fullback commits, you shift across to cover. In this system the covering center back slides to the ball and the fullback rotates in behind you.

Out of Possession: Dominate aerial and ground duels in a duel-heavy system. Delay-and-deny discipline: no rash lunges, direct the attacker away from goal. Stay tight to your partner and to the fullbacks (6 to 8 yards, no gaps through the middle). Hold the back line of the block as one connected unit.

Fullbacks · #2 & #3 · 4-4-2

In Possession: Relentless up-and-back engine. Overlap outside the wide mid or underlap into the half-space to deliver crosses. You are a real source of width and service in this system. When the wide mid holds width, you go inside; when they come inside, you go outside.

Out of Possession: Strong 1v1 defending to stop service at the source. When they send an overlapping runner, stay on the ball carrier and do NOT chase the overlap. If the ball is played to the overlap, the covering center back shifts across to it and you rotate inside to fill the center back spot. On the weak side, tuck inside to keep the back four compact and balanced.

Central Midfielders · #6 & #8 · 4-4-2

In Possession (#6): Ball retention, tempo control, recycling. You are the ball-side pivot in build-out: check in to receive on the half-turn when the ball is on your side, then find the forward routes. Keep us ticking and calm.

In Possession (#8): Box-to-box engine. Drive play forward and arrive late in the box for second balls and crosses. When you check in ball-side, receive facing forward and hit the wide mids or the far switch.

Central Midfield Equilibrium: Never both forward. When one of you steps up to join the attack or crash the box, the other stays behind the ball as the anchor to lock down the counter. This rule is non-negotiable.

Out of Possession: Form a solid central shield: win second balls and intercept the passing lanes into the strikers' feet. Stay tight (6 to 8 yards) and deny the middle. This is the heart of the compact block.

Wide Midfielders · #7 & #11 · 4-4-2

Different job from the 4-3-3. Here you are a MIDFIELDER, not a wide forward. You have two full jobs, attack and defend, and holding both is what makes this system work.

In Possession: 1v1 bravery and accurate crossing. Pin the opponent's fullback to create width, or make the diagonal inside run to find the half-space pocket. In the final third, attack the box with coordinated timing: near post, far post, edge of the 18.

Out of Possession: High work-rate is the price of admission. Track the opponent's fullback, double down out wide with your fullback to set the touchline trap, and most importantly, tuck into the central line to complete the bank of four. When the ball is on the far side, you are almost a third central midfielder.

Strike Partnership · #9 & #10 · 4-4-2

You are a PAIR, never on the same horizontal line. One checks short to link (target), one runs the channel (runner), and you constantly rotate who does which.

In Possession (Target/Link): Back-to-goal hold-up play. Receive, protect the ball, and lay it off to the runner or an arriving midfielder. Attract a center back out to open the space behind.

In Possession (Runner): Play on the shoulder of the last defender. Exploit the space behind that your partner's drop just opened. Your blind-side runs stretch the back line and are our first attacking thought when we win it.

Out of Possession (First Line of Defense): You set the pressing traps. Screen the opponent's holding midfielder (their 6) to block the central lane, and funnel play into the wide channels for the touchline trap. One presses the ball-carrying center back on a curved run to cut the switch; the other drops to cover.

#10 marking note: Our #10 carries the responsibility of marking the opponent's #6 unless directed otherwise. When we defend, that is your reference: deny their pivot.

Goalkeeper · #1

In Possession: You start every attack. Read the press before the ball arrives and make the decision: distribute with purpose, not panic. Be a composed outlet for back passes. Use your footwork as a weapon. Initiate attacks quickly from saves and crosses with accurate distribution to the right target.

Out of Possession: Organize the defensive line constantly; your voice sets the shape. Cover the space behind defenders as the team pushes up. Command your box on aerial balls. Call off the back four clearly and early. Your composure and communication under pressure sets the tone for the whole team.

Right Back · #2

In Possession: Provide width and depth on the right flank. Read when the winger cuts inside; that's your trigger to overlap into the space they've vacated. Tuck into the half-space when the ball is on the opposite side. Connect with the 6 and 8 as a third-man option in the build. Drive forward when the channel is open.

Out of Possession: Stop crosses at the source. Stay compact and connected to the center backs. When you're defending your wide channel and they send an overlapping runner, stay on the ball carrier and do NOT chase the overlap. If the ball is played to the overlap, the covering center back shifts across to it and you rotate inside to fill the center back spot. In the mid block, hold your zone. In emergency box defense, grab your mark and defend it.

Left Back · #3

In Possession: Provide width and depth on the left flank. Read when the winger cuts inside; that's your trigger to overlap into the vacated wide channel. Tuck into the half-space when the ball is on the opposite side. Be a reliable outlet in the build and a threat in the final third when the lane opens.

Out of Possession: Stop crosses at the source. Stay compact and connected to the center backs. When you're defending your wide channel and they send an overlapping runner, stay on the ball carrier and do NOT chase the overlap. If the ball is played to the overlap, the covering center back shifts across to it and you rotate inside to fill the center back spot. In the mid block, hold your zone. In emergency box defense, grab your mark and defend it.

Center Backs · #4 & #5

In Possession: Split wide to the edges of the box to receive and draw the press. Composure on the ball is non-negotiable; your calm starts the attack. Look to break lines with passes into the midfield windows. Scan before the ball arrives. Know your next pass before you receive it. Join the attack when the channel opens. You have permission to carry forward.

Out of Possession: Command the defensive line and communicate constantly. Delay first when space is behind you; do not dive in. Direct the opponent away from goal, then find the moment to engage. Win aerial duels and clear with authority. In the mid block, move as a connected unit. In emergency box defense, grab your mark, defend your space, and clear your lines first.

Holding Midfielder · #6

In Possession: You are the connection between the back four and the attack. Receive with an open body, scan before the ball arrives, and know where your next pass is going. Serve as the primary relief valve when the fullbacks are pressed. Look to break lines into the 8 and 10's half-space pockets. In build-out you are the pivot: Plan B lives and dies with your ability to receive, turn, and play forward immediately. Be the third-man option when the ball goes wide.

Out of Possession: Protect the back four. Maintain positional discipline: stay connected, never more than 10-12 yards from the defensive line. Intercept passes aimed at the opponent's striker. Compete for second balls. Work hard to stay in line or behind the ball in every transition; if you're caught high, you've exposed the back four.

Central & Attacking Midfielders · #8 & #10

In Possession (#10): Operate higher between the opponent's lines, in the window behind their press. Receive on the half-turn, already scanning, already knowing where you're going. Your first thought is always forward. In build-out you drop into the pocket space to connect: receive, turn, and penetrate. Combine quickly with wide players and the 9. You are the creative spark.

In Possession (#8): You are the engine. Box to box. Receive from the 6 and drive the attack forward. Read when to change the pace, when to slow it down, and when to go. Your energy and work rate sets the standard for the entire team. Make yourself available constantly.

Out of Possession (#8): Track back immediately to support the 6. Get in line or behind the ball in transition, no exceptions. Your defensive work rate enables the team's pressing shape.

Out of Possession (#10): If the ball is lost on your strong side, track back immediately. On the weak side, identify runners making counter-attack runs and cut them off in transition. In the mid block, work with the 9 to screen the opponent's pivot and trigger the press when the cue fires.

Wingers · #7 & #11

In Possession: When the ball is on your side, heels on the touchline. Your width is what stretches their back four and creates half-space pockets for the 8 and 10. Read when to cut inside and when to hold width. Take on 1v1s in the final third: be brave, be direct. Deliver crosses on the ground to the front post. Weak-side winger: track the play, tuck into the central channel, and be ready for a turnover or a switch of play arriving at speed.

Out of Possession: Press the opponent's fullbacks immediately. Your press angle should cut off the pass back to their center backs. Track back to support your fullback in wide defensive situations. When we lose the ball, you're the first to chase it. In the mid block, hold your horizontal line and don't get pulled out of shape by runners.

Center Forward · #9

In Possession: Your job starts before the ball arrives: pin the opponent's back line deep with smart movement. Keep them honest. Don't stand next to defenders; make them find you. When you receive facing your own goal, hold the ball and combine with the 8 or 10 arriving in support. Make runs in behind to exploit space. Arrive at the penalty spot for crosses. Follow every shot; the rebound is yours if you're there.

Out of Possession: You start the press. Your angle of approach sets the direction the whole team defends. Approach on a curve to cut off the pass back to the goalkeeper and force play to one side. Block the opponent's pivot. In the mid block, be patient: wait for the pressing trigger, then commit fully. In the high press, you are the spark that ignites the team.

What to Watch For

In Possession
  • Watch the shape of the back 4 during build-out. Are they providing the right width and depth in support?
  • How is our midfield movement during possession? Are we forming a triangle and providing angles of support?
  • Are the forwards creating the right width and height to stretch the opponent's back line?
  • When we win the ball in transition, if we can play forward are we positioned to do so? If we can't, are we forcing the ball on the dribble or forcing it up the field?
Out of Possession
  • Is our press set up correctly on opponent goal kicks?
  • Are we recognizing the pressing triggers and responding as a pack?
  • When the press has been beaten, are we getting into our compact defensive shape?
  • How is our back 4 movement? Are we connected and balanced?
